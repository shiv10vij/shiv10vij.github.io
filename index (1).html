About me

At the intersection of cancer biology and therapeutic discovery.

I'm a PhD candidate in Pharmaceutical Sciences at The Ohio State University, working in Dr. Rajgopal Govindarajan's laboratory. My research focuses on identifying metabolic vulnerabilities in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), particularly the connections between oncogenic KRAS signaling, nucleotide biosynthesis, and treatment resistance.

I combine cell-based pharmacology, molecular biology, preclinical models, and transcriptomic/metabolomic analyses to understand therapeutic response and develop rational combination strategies. I am interested in connecting rigorous mechanism-driven research with translational drug development.

Research focus

Questions driving my research

From pathway biology to preclinical therapeutic development, my research focuses on understanding how cancer cells, particularly in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), adapt to metabolic stress and targeted therapies.

01

Targeting nucleotide metabolism

Understanding DHODH-dependent de novo pyrimidine biosynthesis and nucleoside salvage as vulnerabilities in pancreatic cancer.

02

KRAS signaling & metabolic adaptation

Investigating the interplay between KRAS signaling and cellular metabolism to understand metabolic reprogramming, tumor progression, and therapeutic vulnerabilities.

03

Resistance & translational therapeutics

Characterizing acquired resistance and rational treatment combinations using molecular and preclinical models.
